Resisting Refutation

Does the way a belief was formed decide whether it can be corrected? A post-decision evidence paradigm in the Control Detection Task.

Simon Knogler · LMU Munich
Adapts Rollwage, Dolan & Fleming (2018, Current Biology) · Task: Wen et al.
Status: built & bot-verified · pre-data · design fixed for preregistration
Background

The dissertation in one sentence

How do beliefs about the precision of our own control shape the sense of agency and the confidence we place in it — and does that depend on whether the system runs in a prediction-based or a regularity-based mode?

Prediction mode (0°). A well-learned forward model. Judgement = match/mismatch against a model you already trust. Precise prior; sharp sensitivity to prediction error; sensory attenuation.
Regularity mode (90°). The model is invalid; control is inferred from the ongoing correlation between hand and object. Weak prior; confidence "seldom reaches maximum"; by definition an updating mode.

Dual-mode theory of control: Wen et al. 2021–2024 · precision weighting: Friston 2009; Yon & Frith 2021. WP1 (behaviour) and WP2 (EEG) manipulate expectations before the decision; WP3 manipulates evidence after it.

The question

Confirmation bias where nothing is at stake

Belief perseverance is usually explained by motivation — identity, ego, dissonance. WP3 measures it in a judgement with no identity, no audience and no valence: “which of two circles did I just move?”

  • If the asymmetry appears here, it is a property of inference architecture, not of what the belief is about.
  • The belief is about the self as cause — the most basic self-model there is.
  • And we can manipulate, within a person, how that belief was formed: model-based (0°) vs. evidence-based (90°).
Rollwage measured who resists correction. WP3 asks which kind of belief resists correction.the one-line framing
Template

Rollwage, Dolan & Fleming (2018)

  • Task 1. Dot-number discrimination → confidence (9-point, as subjective P(correct), 0–100 %).
  • Task 2. Decision → a new sample of dots that always points to the true answer (same or ×1.3 strength) → confidence in the initial decision.
  • Measure. Per subject: slope of confidence over evidence level on correct trials (confirmatory) and on incorrect trials (disconfirmatory).
  • Finding. Radicals lower confidence less when new evidence shows they were wrong — reduced disconfirmatory integration; confirmatory integration unchanged.
  • Model. A choice-bias model (a fixed boost for the chosen option) explained individual differences best.
What WP3 keeps: the two-task structure, the 9-point probabilistic scale, evidence that always points to the truth, the quadratic scoring rule, the exclusion criteria.
What WP3 changes: the belief is an agency judgement; the evidence is self-generated (you move again); the contrast is within-subject (0° vs 90°) instead of between radicals and moderates.
Paradigm

The Control Detection Task

Two identical black circles. One blends the participant's mouse movement with a pre-recorded trajectory at control proportion prop; the other replays an unrelated trajectory. After ~5 s: which one did you control? (a = left, s = right). A 1-up-2-down staircase holds accuracy at 70.7 % — the point of maximal uncertainty, where evidence has room to move the judgement.

Paradigm

Same stimulus, one rotation apart

At 0° the circle follows the hand directly — the forward model applies. At 90° the motion is rotated: prediction fails, but the temporal regularity (onset, offset, speed) is preserved, so control can still be detected from correlation. Both modes are staircased to the same accuracy → objective difficulty is matched.

The key mechanic

The participant's own error decides what the evidence means

Not confounded with the stimulus. Confirming and disconfirming samples come from the same distribution; what makes one or the other is whether the participant happened to be right — at 70.7 %, largely internal noise.
Ground truth on every trial. The normatively correct direction of the update is known — something impossible for real-world beliefs. “Bias” can be defined as a shortfall, not just a difference.

Hypotheses

Three predictions and one honest counter-prediction

  • H1 — Asymmetry. Confirming evidence is weighted more than disconfirming (βd < βc), replicating Rollwage in an agency judgement.
  • H2 — Mode (core). The shortfall on disconfirmation is larger at 0° than at 90°; confirmatory integration is the same in both. Beliefs born of a precise internal model resist refutation.
  • H3 — Clinical bridge. Delusional ideation (PDI, esp. conviction) predicts resistance to disconfirmation, most at 0°.
  • Secondary. Task-1 meta-d′ predicts sensitivity to post-decision evidence (Rollwage).
The counter-predictionThe same dual-mode theory says the prediction mode is hyper-sensitive to prediction errors — read literally, 0° should react more to refutation. The resolution is the level: online error sensitivity (first-order, during control) vs. revision of a committed belief afterwards (second-order). H2 bets on the second; the design decides between them, and the reverse result is a finding, not a failure.

Grounds for H2: prior precision (Fletcher & Frith 2009), the regularity mode being an updating mode by construction (Wen), commitment weight (Rollwage 2020: initial confidence gates disconfirmatory processing; Talluri et al. 2018: choice-consistent evidence is actively over-weighted).

Predictions

Why the raw index cannot be trusted simulated

Same simulated data, opposite verdictsThe naive index βc − βd comes out −0.09 at 0° and −0.90 at 90° — i.e. more asymmetry in the regularity mode, the reverse of the hypothesis — even though the model has a strong bias built in at 0° (wd = 0.42 vs 0.88).

Cause: higher baseline confidence at 0° sits nearer the scale ceiling; the logit→scale map is sigmoid, so the same evidence moves confidence less upward and more downward. A perfectly rational observer shows small βc and large βd at 0° by itself.

Consequence: bias = shortfall against the ideal observer (+2.51 vs +0.39 confidence points on strong disconfirmation). The Bayes reference model is not a refinement — it decides the sign of the result. Built before data; preregistered.

Predictions

The most concrete single observable simulated

People see the refutation and stay on their side.

Because values below 5 express a change of mind, the effect can be shown without any regression: the share of incorrect, strongly-refuted trials on which confidence crosses the midpoint. Belief perseverance as a countable event, not a coefficient.

Mechanism

Slope or intercept? Two accounts, two signatures

Rollwage's winning model was choice bias — commitment adds a fixed bonus regardless of evidence strength — not asymmetric weighting. Weighting flattens the slope; commitment shifts the intercept. Three evidence levels separate them. For the prediction mode the intercept version is arguably the sharper hypothesis: a commitment derived from a trusted internal model should weigh more than one assembled from observation. Both are also separable from a mere reporting bias: the scoring rule pays honest reports, and the bias sits in the belief.

Clinical bridge

Delusional ideation and willingness to revise simulated

  • PDI (Peters et al. 1999/2004): delusional ideation in the general population; yes/no items + distress, preoccupation, conviction.
  • Conviction is the theoretically closest subscale — it is resistance to doubt. Preregistered as primary; total score and the others exploratory.
  • Prediction: PDI × mode interaction — a steeper negative slope at 0°.
  • Why it matters: delusions of control are an agency disturbance and a refutation-proof belief. Two-factor theory keeps these apart; if disconfirmatory β scales with PDI inside the agency domain, the two factors may be one inference failure (Fletcher & Frith 2009).

Falsification

What would count against the hypothesis

Only ① supports H2. ② is still publishable — confirmation bias without motivation, in an agency judgement — but leaves the dual-mode question open. ③ (optimal revision in both modes) would question the paradigm's sensitivity. ①-reversed (0° revises more) is the counter-prediction on slide 10 and would support the online-sensitivity reading of dual-mode theory.

Take-home

Three sentences to remember

1 · Whether a belief can be corrected may depend not on what it is about but on how it was formed.

2 · The same person, the same stimulus, the same difficulty — only the mode differs, and only in the prediction mode does the refutation bounce off.

3 · Measured against an ideal observer, in a judgement about one's own action, with ground truth on every trial.
